## Huntsville's Climate and Why It Matters for HVAC

Huntsville sits at the base of the Cumberland Plateau, creating what weather forecasters call the Tennessee Valley Effect. Summer humidity regularly hits 75–85% relative humidity even when temperatures are in the mid-80s. The actual heat index — what it feels like — routinely pushes above 100°F from June through August.

**What this means for AC systems**: Your air conditioning isn't just cooling air — it's also dehumidifying it. A system that's properly sized removes moisture efficiently. An oversized system cools fast but short-cycles, never running long enough to pull humidity out. You feel cool but clammy. Proper load calculation (Manual J sizing) is non-negotiable in Huntsville.

**What this means for heating**: Huntsville averages around 29 cold nights per year with lows below 32°F, but hard freeze events are regular through January and February. Heat pumps lose efficiency below 35°F — at that point, auxiliary electric strips or a gas furnace backup takes over. Systems installed in Huntsville should be rated for the actual temperature range.

## Heat Pumps vs. Gas Furnace: The Huntsville Calculus

This is the most common debate for Huntsville homeowners replacing systems:

**Heat pump advantages**: Single system for both heating and cooling. No gas line needed. In mild winters (most of Huntsville's season), more efficient than gas. Modern cold-climate heat pumps (rated to -13°F) handle Valley winters well.

**Gas furnace + AC advantages**: More aggressive heating on hard-freeze days. If you have natural gas service, operating costs are often lower in deep winter. Separate equipment means independent failure modes.

**The honest answer for Huntsville**: Either works well. Heat pump makes more sense for homes without gas service or in well-insulated newer construction. Gas furnace makes more sense for older homes with higher heating loads and existing gas infrastructure. Either way, proper sizing matters more than system type.

## HVAC Contractor Licensing in Alabama

Alabama requires HVAC contractors to hold a mechanical contractor license issued by the State Licensing Board for General Contractors (SLBGC). Additionally, any contractor handling refrigerants must hold EPA Section 608 certification.

Verify any contractor at [genco.alabama.gov](https://www.genco.alabama.gov) before signing a service agreement. Unlicensed HVAC work voids manufacturer warranties and creates issues with homeowner's insurance claims.

## When Repair Makes Sense vs. Full Replacement

The 5,000 Rule: Multiply the system's age by the repair cost. If the result exceeds $5,000, replacement is typically the better financial decision.

Example: 12-year-old system + $500 repair = $6,000. That tips toward replacement.

Other factors that push toward replacement:
- System is R-22 refrigerant (phased out; increasingly expensive to service)
- Multiple repair events in the last two years
- SEER rating below 13 (current minimum is 15 SEER2 in the Southeast)
- Comfort complaints that repair doesn't resolve

## What to Expect on a Service Call

A legitimate HVAC service call in Huntsville follows a specific pattern:
1. **Diagnostic fee** ($75–$150) — covers the technician's time to find the problem; typically applied toward repair
2. **Upfront repair quote** — before any work starts
3. **Parts and labor quote** — separately itemized
4. **Warranty confirmation** — reputable companies warrant labor (typically 30–90 days) and manufacturer warranty on parts

Be wary of companies that waive the diagnostic fee but charge high parts markups, or that recommend full system replacement on systems under 10 years old without detailed explanation.
