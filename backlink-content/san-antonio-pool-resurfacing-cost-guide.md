# Pool Resurfacing in San Antonio: 2026 Cost Guide (With Real Local Prices)

San Antonio has more swimming pools per capita than almost any other Texas city. With 100+ days above 90°F each year, a functioning pool isn't just a luxury — it's practically a public health amenity. But the same sun that makes pools so valuable also destroys them. If your pool's plaster is rough, stained, or cracking, you're probably thinking about resurfacing. Here's what it actually costs in San Antonio in 2026, and what drives those prices up or down.

## What Pool Resurfacing Costs in San Antonio

San Antonio sits in the middle of the Texas pool resurfacing market — more affordable than Austin (whose labor costs run 10–15% higher), in line with Dallas, and slightly above Houston rates.

These ranges assume a standard residential pool of 10,000–15,000 gallons (roughly 400–500 sq ft of surface). Larger freeform pools, pools with attached spas, or pools with extensive tile and coping will run higher.

## Why San Antonio's Geology Changes the Math

Here's something most cost guides skip: San Antonio sits on the Edwards Plateau. The underlying geology is predominantly limestone and caliche — calcium carbonate-rich rock that releases minerals into groundwater and affects pool chemistry in ways that accelerate surface wear.

San Antonio's water supply comes largely from the Edwards Aquifer, with calcium hardness typically running 200–300 mg/L. Hard water deposits calcium scale on pool surfaces over time, appearing as white crusty buildup on tile and plaster. This scaling:

- Accelerates plaster roughness (rough plaster = higher chlorine demand = more chemical costs)
- Makes quartz and pebble surfaces meaningfully more cost-effective over a 10-year horizon
- Increases the importance of regular acid washing (every 3–5 years) and proper water chemistry management

The caliche soils throughout Bexar County also shift and settle with weather changes — dry summers followed by wet winters create expansion/contraction cycles that can stress pool shells. Pool cracks in SA are often traced to soil movement rather than material failure alone. Any resurfacing quote should include a structural assessment of the shell.

## When Does Your Pool Actually Need Resurfacing?

Not every pool problem requires full resurfacing. Here's a quick triage:

**Needs resurfacing (not just repair):**
- Rough surface that scratches feet or swimsuits
- Persistent staining that acid washing can't remove
- Multiple cracks (especially if water loss is detectable)
- Blistering or delaminating plaster (bubbles or flaking sections)
- Plaster age over 10–12 years with white plaster, 15+ years with aggregate

**May only need spot repair or acid washing:**
- Single small crack (under 1/4 inch, not leaking)
- Calcium scaling without surface degradation
- Fading color without structural issues

**Questions to ask any contractor:**
- Will you do a full structural assessment before quoting?
- Do you drain completely or use underwater patching?
- What's the curing protocol and how long before I can swim?
- Do you handle permits? (Bexar County requires permits for pool renovation work)

## Neighborhoods and What to Expect

Pricing varies modestly across San Antonio's geography, mostly based on crew travel time and local competition:

**Northside (Stone Oak, Alamo Ranch, Helotes)** — Most competitive market; several established resurfacing contractors in this corridor, prices at the lower end of ranges.

**Alamo Heights, Terrell Hills, Olmos Park** — Older pool stock, more freeform designs with complex tiling; expect mid-to-upper price ranges and longer timelines.

**Hill Country corridor (Boerne, Bulverde, Fair Oaks Ranch)** — Travel surcharges common; premium finish popular; budget for 10–15% above base SA rates.

**South Side and Southtown** — Competitive pricing, but fewer pool-specialized crews; verify TDLR license carefully.

## TDLR Licensing: Don't Skip This Step

Texas requires pool contractors to be licensed by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). A Class A Contractor license covers new pool construction and major renovation; Class B covers service and repair only. For resurfacing, you want a licensed Class A contractor.

Verify any contractor at [tdlr.texas.gov](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov) before signing anything. Unlicensed work voids your homeowner's insurance coverage for related damage and creates liability issues if you sell the home.

## What's Included (and What's Not) in a Typical Quote

A complete resurfacing job should include:

✅ Drain and prep (typically 1–2 days)
✅ Surface preparation (chipping, acid washing, or bead blasting the old surface)
✅ Shell inspection and crack repair
✅ Application of new finish material
✅ Startup chemistry and initial water balance
✅ Equipment check (pump, filter, returns)

Watch for these common add-ons that should be disclosed upfront:
- Tile replacement (worn or cracked waterline tile often replaced during resurfacing; $15–45/linear foot)
- Coping repair or replacement ($30–80/linear foot)
- Equipment upgrades (if the pump or filter is flagged during inspection)
- Permits (Bexar County requires them; some contractors include, some don't)

## Timeline Expectations

A typical San Antonio pool resurfacing project runs:
- **Day 1–2:** Drain, surface prep, crack repair
- **Day 2–3:** New surface application (plaster goes in one day; pebble takes 1–2 days)
- **Days 3–7:** Curing period (pool fills slowly, water chemistry monitored daily)
- **Day 7–10:** Pool ready for swimming

The August/September peak season adds 2–4 weeks to scheduling. If you're planning a resurfacing, book in late February or March before the summer rush.

## Bottom Line

For a standard SA backyard pool, budget $5,000–$8,000 for white or colored plaster, $7,000–$10,000 for quartz, and $9,000–$12,000 for premium pebble finishes. Get three quotes, verify TDLR licensing on each, and ask specifically about their experience with Edwards Aquifer water chemistry and caliche soil conditions. Those two San Antonio-specific factors will tell you quickly whether a contractor knows your market.
