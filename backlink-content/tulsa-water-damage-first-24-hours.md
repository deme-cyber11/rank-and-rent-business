# Water Damage in Tulsa? Here's Exactly What to Do in the First 24 Hours

Oklahoma storms don't give you much warning. One hour you're watching a line of thunderstorms roll in from the west on radar; three hours later you've got water coming through your roof, seeping under your back door, or backing up through the floor drain in your basement. If you're in Tulsa and you've got water where it doesn't belong, this guide walks you through what to do — hour by hour — to protect your home, your health, and your insurance claim.

## Why the First 24 Hours Are Everything

Water damage compounds fast. Within 24 to 48 hours, mold begins colonizing wet building materials. In Tulsa's summer humidity — routinely above 70% from May through September — that timeline can be even shorter. What starts as a fixable water extraction job turns into a mold remediation project if you wait. The difference in cost between acting in hour two versus day three can be $10,000 or more.

## Hour 1: Safety First, Then Document Everything

**Before you touch anything:**

- Cut power to any areas with standing water. Water and electricity are a lethal combination. If your main panel is dry and accessible, flip the breakers for affected rooms. If the panel itself is wet or in a flooded area, don't touch it — call the utility company or a licensed electrician.
- Turn off the water main if the source is a burst pipe, failed appliance, or active roof breach you can't stop.
- If the water source is a sewer backup or sewage-contaminated flood, treat it as a biohazard. Don't walk through it without waterproof boots and gloves.

**Document before you clean:**

Your insurance adjuster wasn't there when it happened — your photos are. Take video of every affected room, walking slowly and narrating out loud what you're seeing. Capture:
- Water depth (use a ruler or nearby object for reference)
- The source of water (visible pipe break, soaked ceiling, flooded entry point)
- Wet furniture, flooring, drywall, and personal property
- Timestamps on your phone camera (don't edit the files later)

Don't throw anything away yet. Damaged items are part of your claim.

## Hours 1–4: Stop the Source, Start Removing Water

If the water is still coming in, stop it first:
- Shut off the supply line to the affected fixture (washing machine, water heater, dishwasher)
- Place buckets under active drips
- Use sandbags, towels, or plastic sheeting to divert water away from finished areas

Once the source is stopped, start removing standing water. A wet/dry shop vac works for smaller amounts. For significant flooding — anything over an inch across a large area — professional extraction equipment is far faster and more effective. Industrial truck-mounted extractors can remove hundreds of gallons per hour.

**What to move out of the water:**
- Rugs and area rugs (they hold moisture and harbor mold)
- Furniture legs (put aluminum foil or wood blocks under metal/wood legs to prevent rust and tannin stains on flooring)
- Electronics, documents, photos, and irreplaceable items
- Clothing and textiles

**What NOT to move:**
- Built-in cabinets, flooring, or structural elements until an adjuster or contractor has documented them
- Anything that's clearly a total loss — photograph it in place first

## Hours 4–12: Ventilation and Drying Begins

Open windows if the outdoor humidity is lower than indoors (use a cheap hygrometer — it's worth the $15). Run your central AC on a cool setting; air conditioning dehumidifies as it cools. If you have box fans, point them outward from windows to exhaust humid air.

If you have a dehumidifier, get it running. A single residential dehumidifier is rarely enough for a serious water event, but it helps in small areas.

**Lift carpet if you can.** Carpet padding is a mold sponge. In most cases, padding cannot be saved — it needs to come out. The carpet itself sometimes can be, depending on contamination level and how quickly it's dried.

## Hour 12–24: Get a Professional Assessment

Even if you think you've dried things out, there's likely hidden moisture in wall cavities, under subfloor, and inside cabinets. Restoration professionals use thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to find wet spots invisible to the naked eye. You cannot adequately assess structural moisture with a visual inspection alone.

Tulsa's heavy clay soils, which are prevalent throughout the metro from the River Parks area out to Broken Arrow and Jenks, also create foundation drainage issues that make basement and crawlspace flooding especially prone to hidden moisture problems.

## What Happens When a Restoration Crew Arrives

Here's what professional water damage restoration actually looks like:

1. **Moisture mapping** — Technicians use thermal cameras and penetrating moisture meters to locate every wet area, including hidden cavities
2. **Extraction** — Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water and pull moisture from carpet, padding, and subfloor
3. **Demo as needed** — Saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring may be removed to allow wall cavities to dry
4. **Drying equipment placement** — Commercial air movers and dehumidifiers are staged strategically based on the moisture map
5. **Monitoring** — Crews return daily to check moisture readings and reposition equipment until everything reaches target levels
6. **Antimicrobial treatment** — Applied to any surfaces at risk of mold growth
7. **Documentation** — A complete job file with moisture readings, photos, and drying logs that your insurance company requires

## Oklahoma-Specific Considerations

Tulsa sits squarely in Tornado Alley. The storm season runs roughly March through October, with the worst weeks typically in May and June. Flash flooding is common during heavy rain events because the local soils can't absorb water fast enough — if you live near any of the creeks that feed into the Arkansas River (Bird Creek, Dirty Butter Creek, Mingo Creek), you're at elevated flood risk.

If you're in a 100-year or 500-year floodplain (check FEMA's flood map), standard homeowner's insurance **does not cover flood damage**. Flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) has a 30-day waiting period before coverage kicks in — you cannot buy it the day before a storm.

## The Insurance Question

Call your insurance company as soon as possible — many policies require "prompt notice" of a claim. Get a claim number before any major work begins. Most reputable restoration companies will work directly with your insurance adjuster and help you document the loss properly.

**What is typically covered:**
- Sudden and accidental water damage (burst pipe, appliance failure, roof damage from a storm)
- Resulting damage to structure and personal property (varies by policy)

**What is typically NOT covered:**
- Gradual leaks you ignored (the slow leak under the sink that rotted the cabinet over six months)
- Groundwater flooding without a flood insurance rider
- Sewer backup without a specific sewer backup endorsement
