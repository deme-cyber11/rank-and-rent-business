That Crack Isn't Going Away on Its Own

Orlando homeowners tell us the same story

You've been watching it since last season. Maybe it started as a hairline, maybe it was always a little rough — but now it's wide enough to catch a heel, high enough to scrape a bumper, or bad enough that you think twice every time someone walks across it.

Cracked, heaving, and crumbling concrete is the most common exterior problem homeowners in Orlando call us about — and it's almost always worse underneath the surface than it looks from above. A crack you can see means movement has already happened. A heaved section means the base has failed. And a driveway that looks like it's "just old" may actually be a liability waiting for the wrong day.

The issue isn't just cosmetic. Concrete that's actively deteriorating is a trip hazard. It's a first impression that signals neglect to every guest, buyer, or appraiser who pulls up. And in Central Florida, where intense UV, sandy soil, and heavy summer rain cycles put real stress on flatwork — it doesn't get better without intervention.

What Waiting Actually Costs You

The instinct is to wait — it's a big project, it feels disruptive, you're not sure what it'll cost. That's understandable. But here's what waiting does:

  • Water infiltration. Every crack that opens is a channel for rainwater to get under the slab. Orlando's summer downpours make this worse — water undermines the sandy base fast, and one or two seasons can turn a small crack into a major heave.
  • Base failure. Once the base is compromised, you're not just replacing concrete — you're also paying to excavate, re-compact, and regrade the base material. That adds $1–$2/sq ft to every repair you delay.
  • Trip hazard liability. If a guest, delivery driver, or neighbor trips on your driveway or walkway, you own that liability. A heaved section is a documented hazard.
  • Resale consequences. A driveway in poor condition is one of the first things buyers and inspectors flag. Exterior concrete in rough shape delays sales and invites lowball offers.
  • Limited contractor availability. Concrete crews in Central Florida book out 3–4 weeks in advance during peak season. If you wait until a sale is imminent, you may be waiting longer than you have.

The concrete isn't waiting for you to be ready. It's getting worse.

What Getting Your Driveway Done Actually Looks Like

Most homeowners are nervous about a concrete project because they don't know what to expect. Here's the full process, start to finish.

Base Prep — The Part That Matters Most

This is where cheap contractors cut corners. We grade and compact the base properly, add crushed gravel sub-base, and verify drainage slopes are correct before a yard of concrete gets poured. Central Florida's sandy soil demands this step.

What Orlando Conditions Do to Concrete — And How We Account for It

Central Florida's intense UV, sandy soil (low bearing capacity), and intense summer rain cycles make concrete base prep the critical variable — properly compacted base with rebar is what separates a 20-year driveway from one that cracks in 3 years.

Sandy Soil & Base Failure

Central Florida's sandy substrate washes away and shifts under slabs that weren't built right. We excavate to proper depth and install a well-compacted gravel base — this single step is what separates driveways that last from ones that crack and settle within a few years.

Summer Rain & Drainage

Orlando's summer rainy season dumps several inches in a single afternoon. Every pour we do includes a slope check — water must move away from the slab and foundation, not pool underneath it. Drainage isn't just aesthetic here; it's structural.

Intense UV & Curing

Orlando's 230+ sunny days per year cause concrete to cure too fast when improperly managed — resulting in surface cracking and weakness. We use curing compounds and techniques appropriate for hot-weather pours to ensure proper hydration and maximum strength development.

We've poured concrete across Orlando, Kissimmee, Sanford, Oviedo, and surrounding Central Florida communities. If there's a site-specific condition in your neighborhood — high water table, sandy subgrade, mature tree canopy — we've worked around it before.

Questions Orlando Homeowners Ask Before a Concrete Project

How do I know the new concrete won't crack just like the old one?

Concrete always cracks eventually — the goal is controlling where and when. Properly placed control joints direct cracking to the scored lines, not across the visible slab. Proper base prep prevents the settling and movement that causes uncontrolled cracking. A 4" minimum slab over a compacted gravel base handles residential vehicle traffic for 20–30 years.

Should I replace the whole driveway or just the bad section?

Patching is fine for isolated damage where the surrounding concrete is in good shape. But if the base has failed, patching one section means the next section fails next year. We'll assess what's actually causing the damage and tell you honestly which approach makes sense.

Do I need a permit for driveway replacement in Orlando?

Most driveway replacement projects in Orlando require a permit from the Building Division. Projects that change drainage or involve the right-of-way require additional coordination. We handle permit applications and ensure compliance with local codes — it's included in our process.

How long until I can drive on it?

We recommend keeping vehicles off for 7 days minimum. Full structural strength takes 28 days. Foot traffic is fine after 24–48 hours. In Florida's heat, we're careful about curing — rushing this step is how surface defects happen.

What if tree roots are the problem?

We address root barriers and root removal as part of the scope when needed. Replacing concrete over an active root problem without addressing the root means you'll be replacing it again. We'll tell you upfront what the root situation requires.

Should I get 4-inch or 6-inch thick concrete?

For standard residential use (cars, light trucks), 4 inches on a properly compacted base is sufficient. Choose 6 inches if you regularly park heavy vehicles (RVs, work trucks over 8,000 lbs), have particularly poor soil conditions, or want maximum longevity. We'll recommend the right spec for your situation during the estimate.
